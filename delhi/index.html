Delhi: NCAP, the action plans and this winter

Delhi enters the season with a revised GRAP, a new state winter plan, and a national programme whose Finance Commission funding window ended in March 2026. This page puts the promises, the dates and the money side by side, and says on each line how well it is sourced.

Last checked 5 October 2026. Every row below rests on the outlet or document named beside it. We could not open the CAQM or Delhi government orders themselves, so no row is verified against an order.

What the evidence supports so far. Delhi's measures this winter are mostly announced, not yet shown to be enforced. The money side is thin: the best-sourced figures put Delhi's NCAP spending at 17% of what it received, and the sources disagree on the rest. Delhi's own targets for 2026 are reported in a form that does not add up. Read the sections below before quoting any of it.

Delhi's poor-air days, from CPCB's own bulletins

Days a year on which the Central Pollution Control Board's daily bulletin put Delhi in the Poor, Very Poor or Severe category. The bulletin is a daily AQI for the city, which reports whichever pollutant scores worst that day, so it is a count of bad days and says nothing about the concentration of PM2.5.

NCAP and the money

  • Nationally, Rs 13,415 crore was released under NCAP and Fifteenth Finance Commission grants and Rs 9,929 crore used, 74% of it spent. Road dust took 68% of spending, transport 14%, waste and biomass 12% (CREA, 9 January 2026).
  • The target was up to a 40% fall in PM10, or PM10 of 60 µg/m³, by 2025-26. Of 100 cities with enough data, 23 met the 40% mark and 23 saw PM10 rise (CREA).
  • Delhi's own result against that target is not established here. It needs the city's 2017-18 baseline from the PRANA portal, which we could not read. Two sources we found disagree on whether Delhi improved since 2017, so we state neither.
  • Delhi's 2025 levels. CREA reports an annual PM10 of 197 µg/m³, the highest of any city, and PM2.5 of 96 µg/m³. The 82.2 µg/m³ that JanVayu leads with for New Delhi is IQAir's 2025 figure. They come from different monitors and methods, and we have not reconciled them.

What named experts said about it

As reported by Health Policy Watch in April 2026:

  • An IIT Delhi study found smog guns cut PM2.5 by about 8% after 24 hours, with an effect limited to roughly 100 to 200 metres on either side.
  • Amit Bhatt of the International Council on Clean Transportation said the PUC certificate does not measure NOx or PM2.5, and that the stationary test misses real-world driving emissions.
  • Bhavreen Kandhari warned of weak enforcement capacity and fragmented institutions.
  • Jyoti Pande Lavakare said that without a full firecracker ban from October to March, winter peaks are unlikely to fall much. The Supreme Court has since declined a total ban (see above).

What to watch

  • Stage invocations. CAQM invoked Stage I on 16 April 2026 and again on 19 May 2026, which is on record in JanVayu's progress panel. We have not found a verified Stage I invocation for this October, so we do not state one. The CPCB 4 pm bulletin is the primary source.
  • 1 November. Three measures start together: the entry bar on non-BS-VI vehicles, the reported construction ban, and the goods-vehicle bar. Whether any is enforced at the border is the test.
  • The PRANA portal. Delhi's NCAP expenditure and its PM10 baseline sit there. A direct export would settle the funding and target questions above.
