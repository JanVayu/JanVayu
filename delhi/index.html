Delhi: NCAP, the action plans and this winter

Delhi enters the season with a revised GRAP, a new state winter plan, a seven-year World Bank-backed programme about to begin, and a national programme whose Finance Commission funding window ended in March 2026. This page puts the promises, the dates and the money side by side, and says on each line how well it is sourced.

Last checked 5 October 2026. A row marked official comes from a Press Information Bureau (PIB) release or a Finance Commission report. A row marked press report rests on the outlet named. CAQM's and the Delhi government's own sites could not be read from our build environment, and PRANA's fund tables need a login.

What the evidence supports so far.

The GRAP revision is confirmed by PIB: 48 actions, with diesel generator sets and older inter-city buses now restricted from Stage I. Most of Delhi's other winter measures are announced and not yet shown to be enforced.

Delhi's NCAP money is the weakest line. The official figure is 28% spent as of late 2023, and the best later figure is 17%. A seven-year, Rs 8,300 crore plan starts in September, but we found no World Bank board approval for the 65% loan it assumes. The Sixteenth Finance Commission made no dedicated air-quality award, so the successor to NCAP's largest funding stream is not in its text.

Three figures in circulation are left out. One outlet says the citizen charter grows from 22 to 34 measures; PIB says only that it has "additional actions". Two outlets say 150 more e-buses will take Delhi's fleet to "more than 700", which cannot be squared with the 4,845 e-buses JanVayu's progress panel carries from a 9 July 2026 count. The Supreme Court declined a blanket firecracker ban on 1 October and indicated regulated, time-bound use of green crackers, with barium to be examined on 15 October (press reports: Chenab Times, Organiser). We have not read the order.

Delhi's poor-air days, from CPCB's own bulletins

Days a year on which the Central Pollution Control Board's daily bulletin put Delhi in the Poor, Very Poor or Severe category. The bulletin is a daily AQI for the city, which reports whichever pollutant scores worst that day, so it is a count of bad days and says nothing about the concentration of PM2.5.

NCAP: results and the money

Results

  • The government's count. In a Rajya Sabha reply in the 2026 monsoon session (outlets give 24 July and 6 August), the Minister of State for Environment said 108 of 130 NCAP cities recorded lower annual PM10 in 2025-26 than in 2017-18, 72 by more than 20% and 26 by more than 40%, and that 14 cities met the national PM10 standard, against six in 2017-18 (ChiniMandi, EQ Magazine).
  • CREA's count. Of 100 cities with enough data, 23 met the 40% target and 23 saw PM10 rise (CREA, 9 January 2026). The two look further apart than they are: 26 of 130 is 20% of cities, 23 of 100 is 23%. They use different city sets and sources.
  • Delhi's level, three ways. PM2.5 of 102 µg/m³ for 2025-26 in the Rajya Sabha reply (as reported by EQ Magazine), 96 µg/m³ for 2025 in CREA, and 82.2 µg/m³ for New Delhi in IQAir's 2025 figure that JanVayu leads with. Different periods, monitors and methods; we have not reconciled them. CREA also gives Delhi's 2025 PM10 as 197 µg/m³, the highest of any city.
  • Delhi against its own 2017-18 PM10 baseline is not stated in anything we could read. It sits behind the PRANA login.

What replaces the Fifteenth Finance Commission air-quality grant

  • The old grant. Rs 16,539 crore was allocated to 42 million-plus cities for FY 2020-21 to 2025-26 (official: PIB, 5 February 2024). Delhi, as a million-plus city, was eligible. We could not extract its row.
  • The Sixteenth Finance Commission, whose report was laid on 1 February 2026, records the Environment Ministry's request to continue the air-quality grant for 42 cities, add Kannur, Kochi and Kollam, and include 51 cities of 5 to 10 lakh (paragraph 10.32). Its own local-body award of Rs 7,91,493 crore for 2026-31 (Table 10.5) has basic, performance, special infrastructure (wastewater, Rs 56,100 crore) and urbanisation premium components. In the extracted text of Volume I, the words air, ambient, pollution and NCAP appear only in paragraph 10.32. So the award contains no air-quality component that we can find (report, Volume I).
  • Not checked: the report's annexures and other volumes, and whether the Union government funds a successor outside the Commission. The 2026-27 Union Budget cut the Control of Pollution scheme, which includes NCAP, to Rs 1,091 crore from Rs 1,300 crore in the 2025-26 revised estimates (press report: Business Today).

Swachh Hawa, Swasth Delhi

Launched by the Chief Minister on 3 July 2026 as a seven-year programme, September 2026 to August 2033, costing Rs 8,300 crore across all districts. The financing is described as 65% World Bank (about Rs 5,395 crore) and 35% Delhi government (about Rs 2,905 crore). It targets transport, road dust, construction and demolition waste, solid waste, industry, green cover and water pollution, with a project management unit, monitoring and an integrated command centre (press reports: The Newsmill, Daily Pioneer).

On 11 July the World Bank handed over confirmation of a project preparation grant facility. We found no World Bank board approval of the loan, so the 65% is planned financing and not a commitment. None of the reports we read gives a PM2.5 or PM10 target for it.

What named experts said about it

As reported by Health Policy Watch in April 2026:

  • An IIT Delhi study found smog guns cut PM2.5 by about 8% after 24 hours, with an effect limited to roughly 100 to 200 metres on either side.
  • Amit Bhatt of the International Council on Clean Transportation said the PUC certificate does not measure NOx or PM2.5, and that the stationary test misses real-world driving emissions.
  • Bhavreen Kandhari warned of weak enforcement capacity and fragmented institutions.
  • Jyoti Pande Lavakare said that without a full firecracker ban from October to March, winter peaks are unlikely to fall much. The Supreme Court has since declined a total ban (see above).

What to watch

  • Stage invocations. CAQM invoked Stage I on 16 April 2026 and again on 19 May 2026, which is on record in JanVayu's progress panel. We have not found a verified Stage I invocation for this October, so we do not state one. The CPCB 4 pm bulletin is the primary source.
  • 1 November. Three measures start together: the entry bar on non-BS-VI vehicles, the construction restrictions, and the goods-vehicle bar. Whether any is enforced at the border is the test.
  • The World Bank loan. Board approval would turn Rs 5,395 crore from a plan into a commitment.
  • The air-quality grant. Whether the Union government funds a successor to the Fifteenth Finance Commission grant, and what Delhi receives.
