Curated Highlights & Key Moments

Delhi’s Air Is Eating a World Heritage Site
IIT Roorkee & IIT Kanpur · Humayun’s Tomb, Delhi
Research
A black crust built up over years of polluted air is accelerating the decay of the 16th-century tomb’s sandstone. The crust is mostly gypsum — formed when sulphur dioxide in the air reacts with calcium-rich dust and moisture — and it traps soot, dust and metal particles from vehicles, construction, road dust, biomass burning and industry into a darkening layer. The researchers recommend periodic removal of the crust.
3 August 2026 · International Journal of Architectural Heritage · The Tribune · discussed on r/delhi
Delhi’s First ‘Good’ Air Day in Nearly Three Years
Commission for Air Quality Management · city-wide daily average
Data
On 9 July 2026 Delhi’s daily average AQI fell to 48 — inside the CPCB’s “good” band of 0–50, and the city’s first such day since 10 September 2023. Monsoon rain did it, by below-cloud scavenging: raindrops catch fine particles and carry them down. It did not last. The average was back to 54 the next day, 99 the day after, and Delhi was reading poor again through early August.

This is exactly why the map keeps annual and seasonal figures apart. One clean day is weather. The year is the air.
9–11 July 2026 · CAQM, via WION
“Now we have this toxic monster that is breathing this stuff out, choking us everyday”
Varna Sri Raman · Sukhdev Vihar, ~100 yards from the Okhla plant
Resident
An economist and children’s author, Varna Sri Raman and her husband bought their home in Sukhdev Vihar thinking the bird sanctuary next door would give them some relief from Delhi’s smog. Her rooftop garden overlooks the Okhla waste-to-energy plant, about 100 yards away.
Her 11-year-old son has had severe, persistent bronchitis since he was two, the year they moved in. “If you speak to my son, he’ll say he has had a bad cold since 2018,” she told The New York Times — nebulisers have become a crutch in the household, as they have for many neighbours. Boils and cuts speckle her hands, possible signs of dioxin poisoning according to experts consulted by the paper; she says she can barely type to work on her research papers or the children’s stories she loves writing.
The family invested its life savings in the house and would have to sell at a huge loss. One year an explosion shook one of the plant’s boilers and ash rained down on the neighbourhood for 24 hours. Protests were once common; demonstrations in Delhi have since been clamped down on.
“The state that this country is in right now, it’s impossible to get Hindus to join with Muslims to fight. But Jindal’s pollution doesn’t affect me any different than a Muslim mother.”
Quoted in “Is a ‘Green’ Revolution Poisoning India’s Capital?”, Maria Abi-Habib, The New York Times, 9 November 2024
What the Testing Found at Okhla
Sukhdev Vihar, Haji Basti and Tajpur Pahari · South Delhi
Investigation
A New York Times investigation (Maria Abi-Habib, 9 November 2024) tested air and ash around the Jindal-run Timarpur-Okhla waste-to-energy plant and reported cadmium at 19×, manganese at 11× and arsenic at 10× US EPA safe limits — concluding that roughly a million people around the plant are exposed to emissions carrying cadmium, lead and arsenic. Residents of Sukhdev Vihar, whose colony shares a wall with the plant, have been in legal battle over it for some fifteen years.
“People have sold their houses or moved away to better neighbourhoods for health concerns.” — Dhruv Ahuja, Sukhdev Vihar
Sarita Devi, in Tajpur Pahari, describes coughing that starts when she gets home, and a persistent smell of burning plastic. Mehrunisa Begam, 62, in Haji Basti, points to the dust that settles on children’s faces.
The regulator disagrees. A CPCB official: “Waste-to-energy plants are safe. As per the last inspection, all the boilers in the plant had operational pollution control devices. These reports are creating unnecessary panic.” A 2020 CPCB–DPCC report found heavy metals in fly ash and bottom ash mostly “below detection levels”.
Why this sits on JanVayu: our satellite layer is about a kilometre across, so it is good at “what does this district breathe over a year” and blind to a single stack. Okhla is exactly the case a national map cannot see — and the reason fenceline monitoring and resident testimony are not a supplement to the data, they are the part the data misses.
Nov 2024, still contested | ThePrint, on the New York Times investigation
Delhi’s Wettest July in Years Was Also Its Dirtiest Since 2019
CPCB data, reported 30 July 2026
Data
30 July 2026. Delhi averaged AQI 119 across July — its worst July since 2019 (134), and well above last July’s 78. It happened in a month with more rain than normal: Safdarjung logged 254.7 mm against a normal of 201.9 mm, a 26% excess. The month held both extremes, peaking at 261 (the highest July daily maximum in five years) and dipping to 48 — Delhi’s first “good” air day in July in nine years.
“The monsoon witnessed a typical break this season, but instead of lasting five to six days as it usually does, it extended to around 10 to 11 days.” — Mahesh Palawat, Skymet Weather
Why it matters: the monsoon is supposed to be the clean season, and it is the one people point to when they say the air is improving. A wetter-than-normal July that still came out dirtiest in seven years says the rain is doing less of the work than the sources are. You can see the same pattern for your own area on the map — switch the Colour menu to monsoon and then to winter.
30 July 2026 | National Herald / PTI, from CPCB data
67 Cities Missed the NCAP Target — and Two-Thirds of the Money Went on Road Dust
Centre for Research on Energy and Clean Air (CREA)
Research
“Tracing the Hazy Air 2026”, 9 January 2026. Of the 130 cities in the National Clean Air Programme, 51 met the original 20–30% PM10 reduction target and 23 met the revised 40% target — while 23 cities saw PM10 rise. Of 229 cities with PM10 data, 190 breach the national standard; Delhi is highest at 197 µg/m³, then Ghaziabad at 190.
The spending is the part worth arguing about: 68% of NCAP funds went to road dust management, 14% to transport and 12% to waste and biomass burning — leaving under 1% each for industry, household fuel and public outreach. And 40 of the 130 cities still have no source-apportionment study, meaning they are spending money without knowing what their pollution is made of.
9 January 2026 | CREA, Tracing the Hazy Air 2026

Regulator

CREA — Up to 42% of India's PM2.5 Is Made in the Sky
Centre for Research on Energy and Clean Air
Research
A 2026 CREA analysis (NASA MERRA-2, 2024 data) finds up to 42% of India's PM2.5 is secondary — chemically formed in the air from gases, not directly emitted. SO₂ from coal power (India is the world's largest SO₂ emitter) is the dominant precursor, forming ammonium sulphate — about a third of Delhi's PM2.5, up to 49% post-monsoon. Yet ~78% of coal plants were exempted from flue-gas desulphurisation under the July 2025 MoEFCC norms (only ~8% had installed FGD). Dust-first clean-air spending misses most of this. See our Reading List and blog.
2026 | CREA analysis | Down To Earth | energyandcleanair.org
CAQM — First Off-Season GRAP Invocation
Commission for Air Quality Management, Delhi-NCR
Regulator
19 May 2026, 11:48 AM. CAQM invokes GRAP Stage-I across Delhi-NCR at an AQI reading of 208 — the first-ever off-season activation of the Graded Response Action Plan, which was designed for winter inversions. Stage-I had been briefly revoked on 4 May; it returned 15 days later as heatwave-pollution interaction worsened. The activation is a quiet but consequential signal that GRAP enforcement is no longer limited to Oct-Mar. Citizen advocates have argued for year-round triggers since 2022.
19 May 2026 | CAQM Order Index | caqm.nic.in
Lancet Countdown 2025 Launch — India Chapter
Authored by Indian Institute of Public Health (IIPH) team
Research
Annual figure revised upward: 1.72 million Indian deaths per year attributable to ambient PM2.5 (from 1.5M previously) — among the world’s largest national tolls (~a quarter of the global PM2.5 burden). Re-attribution of household biomass deaths and a tightened high-end exposure-response are the main drivers. The chapter explicitly flags that NAAQS at 40 µg/m³ — eight times the WHO 2021 guideline of 5 — understates actual harm.
May 2026 | Lancet Countdown 2025 | thelancet.com/countdown-health-climate

CSE NCAP Five-Year Review
Centre for Science and Environment
Policy review
Of the 131 NCAP non-attainment cities, only ~37 hit the original 20% PM reduction target by the 2024-26 deadline. 64% of NCAP funds went to dust suppression — mechanical sweeping and water-spraying — rather than the combustion sources (transport, residential, industry) that drive most PM2.5. CSE recommends reweighting the funding formula and adding a city scorecard tied to disbursement.

AQI Limitations
Scientific Community
Research Consensus
"AQI's 'masking effect' reports only the dominant pollutant, hiding dangerous levels of others. India's 'satisfactory' AQI (50-100) allows PM2.5 up to 60 µg/m³ — 12× the WHO guideline. Health impact models use PM2.5 concentration, not AQI."
Epidemiological consensus | Multiple studies
