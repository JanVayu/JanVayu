  • Delhi in winter 2025-26: PM2.5 averaged ~163 µg/m³, among the highest of any monitored city, and not a single day met the WHO daily guideline (CREA).
  • Delhi had spent only 43% of the ₹300 Cr 2025-26 Budget allocation for Pollution Control and Emergency Measures (₹129.83 Cr), largely on short-term measures such as mist sprays and anti-smog guns (RTI reply, Pioneer, 26 Feb 2026).
  • India's annual PM2.5 standard (40 µg/m³) is 8 times the WHO's 2021 annual guideline (5 µg/m³). The 24-hour standards differ by 4 times (60 against 15 µg/m³). The national standard (NAAQS) was last updated in 2009.

GRAP and monitoring gaps

  • GRAP was invoked repeatedly through the winter, with long Stage III/IV spells (per CAQM invocation orders; no official aggregate published).
  • Newslaundry found AQI 400+ at street level against 249 at the nearest CPCB station (Safdarjung, Mar 12).
  • The recommendation is that NCAP Phase III use meteorology-adjusted targets rather than raw annual averages.
The NCAP 2026 deadline is close. The target was 40% PM10 reduction. Only 23/100 cities met it. Most cities still far from both national and WHO PM2.5 standards.

Assessment against the World Bank's "Four I's" framework

The World Bank's December 2025 report names four pillars of clean air progress. We assess India's NCAP against each.

Information

"Accessible, reliable data for planning and accountability"

India: CPCB CAAQMS coverage is expanding but gaps remain. Source apportionment studies are complete in only 90 of 130 cities (CREA 2026). JanVayu fills part of the gap in data for citizens.
PARTIAL

Incentives

"Encourage behavioral and investment shift"

India: EV subsidies exist but reach few people. The stubble incentive is weak: Punjab's proposed ₹2,500/acre cash scheme stalled after the Centre declined to fund its share (2024); support is limited to the CRM machinery subsidy. The Ujjwala refill subsidy is insufficient.
WEAK

Institutions

"Coordinate action, ensure compliance"

India: CAQM was created in 2021 but has limited enforcement. States still blame one another instead of coordinating. NCAP city action plans are inconsistent.
WEAK

Infrastructure

"Enable clean energy, transport, waste systems"

India: Metro expansion is under way. EV charging is patchy. Waste management infrastructure is decades behind need. Industrial modernisation is slow.
PARTIAL

Budget and fund spending

NCAP funds (2019-2025)

  • ₹13,415 crore was released to 130 cities (CREA 2026).
  • About ₹9,929 crore (74%) has been spent (CREA 2026). The Ministry's own reply in the Lok Sabha on 15 Dec 2025 (Unstarred Question 2339, tabulated by PRS) gives ₹10,003 crore (75%) used out of the same ₹13,415 crore released. Neither source says why the used figure differs by ₹74 crore.
  • Delhi has spent ₹14.1 Cr, which is 17% of the ₹81 Cr released to the city (Lok Sabha reply of 15 Dec 2025, tabulated by PRS; the ResGov city brief gives ₹81.36 Cr and ₹14.1 Cr) or 14% of the ₹99.77 Cr released (Lok Sabha reply, 2 Feb 2026, as reported by the press).
  • 68% went on road dust control, such as sweeping and sprinkling (CREA 2026).
  • Only 90 of 130 cities completed source apportionment (CREA 2026).

Everyone points to everyone else. CAQM was created in 2021 to coordinate, but has limited enforcement power. Fixing this needs political will across party lines.
How to file an RTI

What is RTI?

The Right to Information Act 2005 gives every Indian citizen the right to request information from government bodies. Use it to ask for air quality data, details of how funds were spent, and action reports.

Where to file

  • CPCB (Central Pollution Control Board) holds national air quality data and NCAP funds.
  • DPCC (Delhi Pollution Control Committee) holds Delhi-specific data.
  • State PCBs hold state-level pollution data.
  • MoEFCC holds policy decisions and national programmes.
  • CAQM holds records on GRAP implementation in Delhi-NCR.

How to file

  • Online: rtionline.gov.in (Rs 10 fee).
  • By post: send it to the CPIO of the relevant department with a Rs 10 postal order.
  • The reply is due in 30 days (RTI Act s.7(1)), or in 48 hours where the information concerns the life or liberty of a person.
  • Appeals: make the first appeal to the First Appellate Authority within 30 days (s.19(1)), and the second to the CIC or State Information Commission within 90 days (s.19(3)).
