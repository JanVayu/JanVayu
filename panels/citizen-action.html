Citizen Action Plan

Emergency Measures to Combat Air Pollution (EMCAP) is a citizens' action plan with legally validated policy recommendations. It has been updated after winter 2025-26 and now covers year-round action. CAQM invoked off-season GRAP Stage-I again on 19 May 2026 (it had also been invoked on 16 April 2026 and in May 2025), and the NGT issued its south-India PM roadmap order (NGT Southern Zone, Chennai, 28 April 2026). Together these point to a shift away from winter-only crisis response.

Delhi/NCR's pollution is a problem of enforcement and political will
Knowledge, behaviour and technology are not what is missing. Awareness alone has not worked. Real solutions need strict enforcement, which causes short-term inconvenience but delivers results.

Vehicles ≈ half of Delhi's internal PM2.5

Transport is the single largest local source, at about 51-53% of Delhi's locally generated PM2.5 on 10-20 November in 2023-25 (CSE analysis of IITM Decision Support System data); the CSE/IITM figure for 1-15 December 2025 is 46%. This is why the only realistic solution is a large shift from private to public transport.

The stubble burning myth

Stubble burning's share of Delhi's PM2.5 is large on some days and small over a season: CSE puts the October-November 2025 average at about 4.2%, and farm fires were 0.2% in December 2025, when PM2.5 still averaged 210 µg/m³ (CSE). Stubble smoke matters on its worst days, but it does not explain Delhi's winter on its own.

Emergency Measures to Combat Air Pollution (EMCAP) Winter action plan

Period: October 15 – March 15 (All NCR) | Model: London ULEZ (roadside NO₂ 54% lower in central London than without it, 2024; TfL), Paris low-emission zones

Transport measures

  • Car-pools of at least three occupants, with dedicated lanes and heavy fines
  • Odd-even rationing that includes two-wheelers, with no exemptions
  • In the central business district, drivers park at edge lots and take electric shuttles
  • Proposed vehicle bans: no new ICE sales (proposed start April 2026, a date now past) and no diesel passenger vehicles
  • More public transport: the DTC fleet from 7,000 to 20,000 buses (all EV), and metro fares halved
  • Vehicle-free Sundays, for emergencies only

Construction & industry

  • A construction holiday: non-essential construction shut down from October to February
  • Immediate shutdown of industrial violators, with no compromises
  • A complete ban on diesel generator sets, with a pollution-emergency power tariff
  • The 50 stretches that generate the most dust resurfaced within 90 days

Open & waste burning

  • No burning of wood, coal or waste, Lohri included
  • A nationwide ban on fireworks (weddings and Diwali), with a heavy purchase tax
  • Putting out fires immediately as a performance target for the fire brigade

Enforcement & governance

  • Monthly reviews by the PM, CM and Chief Secretary, with public dashboards
  • Mandatory quarterly PUC checks, and no fuel for dirty vehicles
  • A monthly public ranking of police on emission reduction

Changing urban transport on the Paris-Singapore model
Core principle: move people rather than vehicles, and cut trips as well as emissions.

Why flyovers fail

  • Shift congestion downstream
  • Encourage longer car trips (induced demand)
  • Create hostile environments underneath
  • Seoul removed the Cheonggyecheon flyover; small-particle pollution there fell about 35% (74 to 48 µg/m³; Landscape Performance Series)

What works

  • Rail-first: Metro + suburban rail + bus integration
  • Walking infrastructure: Continuous footpaths
  • Freeze flyovers; at-grade junctions with signals
  • Congestion pricing in CBDs

Pollution exposure by economic status

In "Who Gets to Breathe Clean Air in New Delhi?" (17 Dec 2020), New York Times journalists tracked the PM2.5 exposure of two Delhi children over a single day (3 December 2019).

Monu, poor (slum near the Yamuna)

  • 24-hour average: 148.9 µg/m³
  • No air purifier, limited ability to stay indoors
  • About five years more life lost than Aamya (NYT estimate: Monu more than 6 years against the then-WHO guideline, Aamya about 1; extrapolated from one day)

Aamya, affluent (Greater Kailash)

  • 24-hour average: 36.6 µg/m³; highest reading about 100 µg/m³, indoors at bedtime
  • Air purifier, AC, can limit outdoor time
  • About 75% lower exposure over the day (36.6 vs 148.9 µg/m³; one day, not a long-term average)
Key insight: In this NYT estimate, which rests on two children, the poorer child loses much more life than the affluent one. Policy discussion centres on middle-class concerns and overlooks the heavier burden on the poor.
