Vehicles ≈ half of Delhi's internal PM2.5

Transport is the single largest local source — about 51-53% of Delhi's locally generated PM2.5 on 10-20 November in 2023-25 (CSE analysis of IITM Decision Support System data); the CSE/IITM figure for 1-15 December 2025 is 46%. This is why the only realistic solution is a massive shift from private to public transport.

The Stubble Burning Myth

Stubble burning's share of Delhi's PM2.5 is large on some days and small over a season: CSE puts the October-November 2025 average at about 4.2%, and farm fires were 0.2% in December 2025, when PM2.5 still averaged 210 µg/m³ (CSE). Stubble smoke matters on its worst days, but it does not explain Delhi's winter on its own.

Emergency Measures to Combat Air Pollution (EMCAP) Winter Action Plan

Period: October 15 – March 15 (All NCR) | Model: London ULEZ (roadside NO₂ 54% lower in central London than without it, 2024; TfL), Paris low-emission zones

Transport Measures

  • Car-Pooling: Min 3 occupants; dedicated carpool lanes with heavy fines
  • Odd-Even: Include two-wheelers; no exemptions
  • CBD Access: Park at edge lots, take electric shuttles
  • Vehicle Bans (proposals): No new ICE sales (proposed start April 2026, a date now past); ban diesel passenger vehicles
  • Public Transport: DTC 7,000 → 20,000 buses (all EV); halve metro fares
  • Sunday Lockdowns: Vehicle-free Sundays (emergency only)

Construction & Industry

  • Construction Holiday: Shutdown non-essential construction Oct-Feb
  • Industrial Compliance: Immediate shutdown of violators—no compromises
  • Diesel Gensets: Complete ban; pollution emergency power tariff
  • Road Dust: Top 50 dust-generating stretches resurfaced in 90 days

Open & Waste Burning

  • Zero Tolerance: No wood/coal/waste burning, including Lohri
  • Fireworks: Nationwide ban (weddings/Diwali); heavy purchase tax
  • Fire Brigade: Immediate extinguishing as KPI

Enforcement & Governance

  • High-Level Oversight: Monthly PM-CM-Chief Secretary reviews with public dashboards
  • PUC Compliance: Quarterly mandatory; deny fuel to dirty vehicles
  • Police Ranking: Monthly public ranking on emission reduction

Transforming Urban Transport — Paris-Singapore Model
Core Principle: Focus on moving people, not vehicles. Reduce trips, not just emissions.

Why Flyovers Fail

  • Shift congestion downstream
  • Encourage longer car trips (induced demand)
  • Create hostile environments underneath
  • Seoul removed the Cheonggyecheon flyover; small-particle pollution there fell about 35% (74 to 48 µg/m³; Landscape Performance Series)

What Works

  • Rail-first: Metro + suburban rail + bus integration
  • Walking infrastructure: Continuous footpaths
  • Freeze flyovers; at-grade junctions with signals
  • Congestion pricing in CBDs

The Class Gap — Pollution Exposure by Economic Status

In "Who Gets to Breathe Clean Air in New Delhi?" (17 Dec 2020), New York Times journalists tracked the PM2.5 exposure of two Delhi children over a single day (3 December 2019).

Monu — Poor (slum near the Yamuna)

  • 24-hour average: 148.9 µg/m³
  • No air purifier, limited ability to stay indoors
  • Impact: about five years more life lost than Aamya (NYT estimate: Monu more than 6 years against the then-WHO guideline, Aamya about 1; extrapolated from one day)

Aamya — Affluent (Greater Kailash)

  • 24-hour average: 36.6 µg/m³; highest reading about 100 µg/m³, indoors at bedtime
  • Air purifier, AC, can limit outdoor time
  • About 75% lower exposure over the day (36.6 vs 148.9 µg/m³; one day, not a long-term average)
Key Insight: In this NYT estimate, which rests on two children, the poorer child loses much more life than the affluent one. Policy discourse centers on middle-class concerns while ignoring the disproportionate burden on the poor.
Source: The New York Times, "Who Gets to Breathe Clean Air in New Delhi?", 17 Dec 2020 (exposure measured 3 Dec 2019)
