The data

Where does JanVayu's air-quality data come from?
Live readings come from public monitoring networks — India's CPCB CAAQMS stations, aggregated via WAQI, plus OpenAQ and community sensors, with forecasts from Open-Meteo (CAMS) and fire detections from NASA FIRMS. Nothing is proprietary; every source is named on the Data Source Selector and in the Data Archive.
How often does the data update?
The live AQI dashboard refreshes every 10 minutes in your browser. Social/news feeds are pre-fetched every few hours; the 5-day forecast follows the model's update cycle. The "as of" time shown on a reading tells you how fresh that specific station is.
Why do CPCB, WAQI, IQAir and my phone's app all show different numbers?
They use different stations, averaging windows (hourly vs 24-hour), and sometimes different AQI scales (India's CPCB scale vs the US EPA scale). A "150" on one is not the same as a "150" on another. JanVayu lets you pick your source and explains the trade-offs in the Data Source Selector.
Is the data real-time and can I trust it?
It's near-real-time (10-minute refresh) and drawn straight from official + community networks, with a freshness guard so dead stations don't surface stale readings as "live". Every statistic on the site is also fact-checked weekly against current primary sources — see the fact-check log.
Can I download the data or use it in my own project?
Yes. The Open Data API is a free, open index of every dataset, plus a CSV of city rankings — free to reuse with attribution (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). Research papers and datasets are in the Data Archive.

Understanding the AQI

What does the AQI number actually mean?
AQI is a single index built from up to six pollutants (PM2.5, PM10, NO₂, SO₂, CO, O₃). Each gets its own sub-index, and the AQI reports only the worst one — so two cities both at "200" can have completely different problems. See the illustrated Understanding AQI panel.
Why does everyone focus on PM2.5?
PM2.5 — particles smaller than 2.5 microns, about 30× thinner than a hair — is small enough to cross from the lungs into the bloodstream and reach nearly every organ. It's the pollutant most consistently tied to death and disease. But over-focusing on it can hide ozone or NO₂ that may be driving your city's AQI on a given day.
What's a "safe" level? WHO vs the Indian standard.
The WHO guideline for annual PM2.5 is 5 µg/m³. India's national standard (NAAQS) is 40 µg/m³ — eight times higher. India's national average is about 48.9 µg/m³ (IQAir 2025), roughly 10× the WHO guideline.
What do the AQI colours / categories mean?
Good → Satisfactory → Moderate → Poor → Very Poor → Severe. From "Poor" upward, sensitive groups (children, elderly, pregnant people, those with heart/lung conditions) should limit outdoor exertion; at "Severe" everyone should. The dashboard's "What to do today" card translates the current number into concrete advice.

Health

How many people does air pollution kill in India?
About 1.72 million deaths a year from ambient (outdoor) PM2.5 (Lancet Countdown 2025, 2022 data). Counting household air pollution too, the total is around 2.1 million (State of Global Air 2024, GBD 2021). It's the world's largest national toll — roughly a quarter of the global PM2.5 burden.
What is the "cigarettes per day" number on the dashboard?
A way to make PM2.5 tangible: Berkeley Earth's rule of thumb is that 1 cigarette ≈ 22 µg/m³ of PM2.5 over a day. So the dashboard converts today's air into an equivalent number of cigarettes. It's an illustration of inhaled particulate dose, not a medical equivalence.
Isn't this just a lung problem?
No. PM2.5 crosses into the blood and is linked to heart attacks and stroke, kidney decline (2026 Chennai–Delhi eGFR cohort), dementia and cognitive loss (~17% higher risk per 10 µg/m³, Lancet Planetary Health 2025), and preterm birth / low birth weight. The Beyond the Lungs panel details the whole-body toll.
Who is most at risk?
Children (they breathe more air per kg and their lungs are still developing), pregnant people, older adults, anyone with heart or lung conditions, and outdoor workers — street vendors, traffic police, gig riders, construction and waste workers. See Occupational Exposure and Health Impact.
Do masks and air purifiers actually help?
Yes, correctly chosen. A well-fitted N95/FFP2 filters fine particles (cloth and surgical masks don't); a HEPA purifier sized to the room lowers indoor PM2.5. The Purifier Calculator and the Indoor Air panel help you size and choose, including low-cost options.
Is it safe to go outside / exercise today?
Open the "Should I Go Outside?" tool — it reads your city's live number and gives real-time advice by activity and risk group. As a rule, reschedule hard outdoor exercise when the air is "Poor" or worse.

Using JanVayu

How do I check my city's air quality?
The dashboard opens on live AQI; use the city selector (or "Near me") to switch cities. The Live Map shows station-level readings across India, and City Rankings shows the cleanest and most polluted right now.
Can I get alerts when the air gets bad?
Yes — open AQI Alerts to set a threshold and get a Web Push notification (delivered even when the site is closed) or a daily email digest. You can also install JanVayu as an app (below).
How do I install JanVayu as an app on my phone?
It's a PWA. On Android/Chrome tap the browser menu → "Install app" / "Add to Home screen"; on iOS/Safari tap Share → "Add to Home Screen". The standalone Ask JanVayu assistant installs the same way.
What is "Ask JanVayu"?
An AI assistant that answers air-quality questions in plain language across 10 languages, with sources — "will it be bad tomorrow?", "is it safe to run?", "what's driving my city's AQI?". Open it from the nav or at janvayu.in/ask.
Which languages does JanVayu support?
The interface translates across Hindi, Tamil, Marathi, Bengali and English, and the assistant answers in 10 languages. The Citizen Testimony wall carries first-person accounts in 13 Indian languages, each with an English translation.

Accountability & action

What are NCAP and GRAP?
NCAP (National Clean Air Programme) sets pollution-reduction targets for non-attainment cities; its 40% PM10 target had a 31 March 2026 deadline, which only 27 of 96 cities with sufficient data met (CREA). GRAP (Graded Response Action Plan) is Delhi-NCR's emergency, stage-by-stage response as AQI worsens. Both are tracked in the Accountability and City Policy trackers.
How do I file an RTI about air pollution?
Use the RTI Assistant — ready-to-use Right to Information templates for pollution accountability, and a one-click "File an RTI" from the City Scorecards that pre-fills your state board and Clean Air Action Plan topic.
Where does the NCAP money go — and is it spent?
The Budget Tracker follows NCAP + 15th Finance Commission grants and per-city utilisation (from CREA / official data). Rows without a verifiable source are marked or removed rather than guessed — the weekly fact-check keeps this honest.
How can I contribute or add my voice?
Add a testimony in your language (via the Citizen Testimony panel or contribute@janvayu.in), tackle a good first issue on GitHub, or suggest a fact for the "Did You Know" wall. See CONTRIBUTING.md.

About JanVayu

Who runs JanVayu, and is it partisan?
JanVayu (जनवायु — "People's Air") is a non-partisan, citizen-led initiative — a public record of India's air-quality crisis, not a campaign. It names problems and sources without endorsing parties. Meet the people behind it on the Team page.
Is it free? What's the licence?
Free, ad-free, and open. Code is MIT-licensed; content and data are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (use with attribution, non-commercially, share-alike). The full source is on GitHub.
How do you make sure the numbers are accurate?
Every statistic and calculator constant is web-verified against current primary sources (Lancet Countdown, IQAir, AQLI, State of Global Air, WHO, CPCB, CREA, NASA) in a weekly automated fact-check that opens a review PR. The latest findings are public in the fact-check log. Spot a wrong figure? Please tell us.
