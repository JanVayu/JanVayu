Household actions by budget

₹0: no-cost actions

  • Mop floors twice a day to trap settled PM2.5.
  • Seal gaps around doors and windows with wet towels or cloth.
  • Avoid frying during the peak pollution hours (6-10 AM).
  • Skip agarbatti and dhoop on high-AQI days, because incense adds PM2.5.
  • Never dry sweep, which puts dust back in the air. Wet mop instead.
  • Gargle with saline water and clean your nose after being outdoors.
  • Always use the chimney or exhaust while cooking.
General household advice; not drawn from a single cited study

₹500-2000: low cost

  • N95 or N99 masks cost ₹50-150 each. Only N95 and above block PM2.5. Cloth masks do not.
  • A DIY air purifier called a Corsi-Rosenthal box (a box fan with MERV-13 filters taped to it) has been shown in lab tests to clean a room's air at rates that can approach small HEPA purifiers (Dal Porto et al., Aerosol Science and Technology, 2022).
  • Indoor plants are pleasant, but a review of the evidence (Cummings and Waring, 2020) found potted plants remove volatile organic compounds far too slowly to matter indoors. Plants have not been tested as PM2.5 filters, so do not rely on them for particles.
  • Door draft stoppers (₹200-400) seal the gap under doors.
  • Cling wrap over windows (₹100) gives a temporary seal on severe days.
  • Ten minutes of steam inhalation a day helps clear the airways.
  • A compost bin (₹500-1000) reduces waste burning.
DIY purifier: Search "Smartair India" for instructions

₹5000+: larger purchases

  • A HEPA air purifier costs ₹8,000-50,000. Check the CADR rating against your room size, and run it around the clock on bad days.
  • An AQI monitor (₹3,000-8,000) shows your indoor and outdoor levels.
  • A car cabin filter costs ₹1,500-3,000. Replace it with a HEPA filter to protect your commute.
  • Ducted AC can take MERV-13 filters.
  • Professional weather stripping for windows costs ₹5,000-15,000.
  • For the severe season, keep one purifier in each bedroom and one in the living area.
  • Induction cooking is better than LPG for indoor air quality.
Purifier sizing (AHAM rule): smoke CADR (CFM) at least two-thirds of the room floor area (sq ft)
Indoor activities for kids

Above AQI 200, outdoor play is harmful. Children breathe faster and inhale more pollutant for each kilogram of body weight. These indoor activities use up their energy:

Physical activity

  • Indoor obstacle course
  • Dance/Zumba videos
  • Balloon volleyball
  • Yoga for kids
  • Hide and seek
  • Indoor bowling (bottles)

Creative play

  • Arts & crafts
  • Building blocks/Lego
  • Playdough/clay
  • Indoor gardening
  • Science experiments
  • Cooking together

Learning games

  • Board games
  • Puzzles
  • Reading time
  • Educational apps (limited)
  • Music/instruments
  • Storytelling

Timing tips

  • Best outdoor window: 12-4 PM (usually lowest AQI)
  • Avoid: 6-10 AM, 6-10 PM (peak hours)
  • Check hourly AQI before deciding
  • Short bursts better than long exposure

Proper Mask Usage

  • Fit test: there should be no gaps around the nose or chin. If your glasses fog, the seal is bad.
  • Replace the mask every 3-5 days of regular use, or when breathing becomes difficult.
  • Children need a kid-sized N95. Adult masks do not seal properly.
  • Facial hair breaks the seal. Clean-shaven gives the best protection.
  • A two-strap mask seals better than an ear-loop one.
  • Avoid touching the mask surface, because contaminants transfer to your hands.
Schools: administrators and parents

What Schools Should Do

  • Keep outdoor PT and games off the timetable when AQI is above 200.
  • Move morning assembly indoors on bad days.
  • Put air purifiers in nursery and KG classrooms at the least.
  • Seal classrooms with weather stripping on windows and door draft stoppers.
  • Plant trees along the roadside boundary.
  • Avoid peak traffic hours for bus routes where possible.
  • Offer a hybrid or online option during severe pollution (AQI above 400).

What parents can demand (legal basis)

  • CAQM order of December 2025: outdoor sports and physical activity to be suspended in Delhi-NCR during adverse air quality (as reported by Deccan Herald)
  • Form a parent WhatsApp group to coordinate pressure.
  • Write to the District Education Officer, who can issue directives.
  • Use an RTI to ask the school for its air quality management plan.

Hospitals and clinics

Clinical preparedness

  • Stock nebulizers, bronchodilators and steroids ahead of the November-December surge.
  • Fast-track patients in respiratory distress on severe AQI days.
  • Consider extended OPD hours during the pollution season.
  • Give N95 masks to staff, especially in emergency.
  • Print handouts on managing pollution at home for patients.
  • Check oxygen supply and concentrator availability.

Infrastructure

  • Critical care units (ICU and NICU) need HEPA-filtered air.
  • Operation theatres should have positive pressure ventilation.
  • Put air purifiers in crowded OPD waiting areas.
  • Fit cabin air filters in ambulances that carry patients.
  • Show real-time AQI in the lobby so patients can see it.
RWAs and housing societies

Immediate Actions

  • Ban leaf burning, use a compost pit instead, and fine violators.
  • Do not allow open waste burning. Report to the MCD if staff burn waste.
  • Limit diesel generator use and switch to gas or solar.
  • Spray common areas with water twice a day to suppress dust.
  • Require dust screens and water spraying at construction sites.
  • Offer incentives for shared transport.
  • Install EV charging points to encourage electric vehicles.

Legal powers and long-term steps

  • RWAs can adopt society rules on burning and diesel generator use, and report violations to MCD or DPCC
  • Plant pollution-resistant trees (Neem, Peepal, Ashoka).
  • A shared community AQI monitor costs ₹8,000-15,000.
  • Negotiate bulk rates on N95 masks for residents.
  • Set up an emergency fund to buy purifiers for vulnerable families.
  • Lobby the MLA or councillor together for local road paving and tree planting.
Rural and urban areas

Rural areas

Main sources: crop burning, biomass cooking, dust roads, brick kilns

  • Alternatives to stubble burning:
    • Happy Seeder (reports put the price at roughly ₹1.5-2.5 lakh; the central government offers financial assistance of 50% of machinery cost, per PIB)
    • In-situ decomposition with PUSA bio-decomposer (free from the government)
  • Cooking: LPG is better than a biomass chulha. PM2.5 from a chulha is roughly equal to smoking 400 cigarettes an hour (Prof. Kirk Smith, UC Berkeley).
  • Report illegal brick kilns to the SPCB. Zigzag technology is mandatory.
  • Demand road paving from the Gram Panchayat. MGNREGA funds are available.
  • Avoid working in the fields early in the morning, when mist traps pollution.
Subsidy: Contact District Agriculture Officer for Happy Seeder

Urban areas

Main sources: vehicles, construction, industry, road dust, waste burning

  • Transport:
    • Metro/public transport when possible
    • Carpool apps (Quick Ride, etc.)
    • WFH during severe pollution
    • EV for next vehicle purchase
  • Report construction sites without dust screens to MCD (call 155305 or use the MCD311 app)
  • Report waste burning through the Sameer app or the MCD helpline.
  • If you can, avoid living near highways and industrial areas.
  • In a car, keep the windows up and the AC on recirculate.
App: "Sameer" for reporting pollution violations
Beyond your home: advocacy

Report violations

  • The Sameer app, from the CPCB, takes reports of pollution sources.
  • The Green Delhi app takes Delhi-specific complaints.
  • The MCD Citizen's Call Center (155305, or the MCD311 app) handles burning and construction dust.
  • The DPCC (dpcc.delhigovt.nic.in) handles industrial violations.
  • Report visibly polluting vehicles to the Traffic Police.

Demand action

  • File an RTI. Templates are in the RTI tab.
  • Write to your MLA or MP, since elected representatives respond to volume.
  • Attend ward meetings and raise air quality in local forums.
  • Share violations with journalists.
  • Support existing public interest litigations.

Community building

  • Use WhatsApp groups to coordinate local clean air initiatives.
  • Organise tree plantation drives with the RWA or school.
  • Share AQI information with neighbours and domestic help.
  • Build a local rideshare community.
  • Help street food sellers get LPG.
