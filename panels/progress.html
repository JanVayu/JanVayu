Cities showing real progress

Only 23 of the 100 NCAP cities with sufficient data met the 40% target, which looks low, but some individual cities have done well. Each card names its own source. They measure different pollutants over different periods, and observed and forecast values appear side by side, so they should not be compared directly.

Varanasi

76%
PM2.5 reduction 2019-2024 (Respirer Living Sciences, Jan 2025)

The largest fall in that report. The 76% is a measured change in PM2.5 from monitor data, 2019 to 2024. It is a different thing from the 40% figure sometimes quoted for Varanasi, which is a PM10 reduction that a Lancet Regional Health SE Asia (2024) model forecast for 2025-26. One is a measured fine-particle fall, the other a forecast for coarse dust, so the two should not be set against each other. We have not verified what caused the PM2.5 fall.

Bareilly

70%+
PM10 reduction (predicted, Lancet SEA 2024)

A statistical forecast, not a measurement. Among the strongest performers in UP's non-attainment cities, alongside Raebareli (58%).

Moradabad

58%
PM2.5 reduction 2019-2024 (Respirer Living Sciences, Jan 2025)

A brass industry hub, second to Varanasi in that report. The causes are not established here.

Caveat

NCAP measures PM10, not PM2.5. The deadlier fine particles from combustion (vehicles, industry, cooking) are harder to reduce than coarse dust. Guttikunda et al.'s 2024 analysis of 2019–2023 data documented "no change in fraction of cities above 150 µg/m³" for PM10 nationally, and TROPOMI satellite data through the same window showed SO2 and NO2 actually increased. Progress is real in some places, but the national picture remains mixed.

Electric mobility is India's strongest clean-air story

This is where the most tangible, independently verifiable progress is happening. Delhi's e-bus fleet reached 4,286 in Feb 2026 (Vahan data, Tribune), 4,845 by Jul 2026 and over 5,000 by 19 Aug 2026. The PM-eBus Sewa scheme could change public transport in 169 cities.

Delhi has India's largest e-bus fleet

4,845 e-buses operational (latest count, 9 Jul 2026, after 300 more were added; electrive); India's largest fleet, having overtaken Maharashtra's 4,001 in Feb 2026, when CM Rekha Gupta flagged off 500 new buses at Ramlila Maidan.

Update, 19 Aug 2026: Delhi's electric bus fleet crossed the 5,000 mark with 200 new buses (Indian Express).

The Delhi government targets 7,000 e-buses by end 2026 and 14,000 by 2028 (as reported by electrive, 9 Jul 2026). A Delhi-Panipat interstate e-bus route has been launched.

E-buses remove tailpipe PM, NOx and CO2. We do not give a tonnes-of-CO2 total because it depends on the fleet replaced (Delhi's was mostly CNG) and on grid emissions (source to be added).

Delhi has 8,849 EV charging points (ThePrint, Jan 2026), with 7,000 more planned across 2026. The city's second EV policy was first expected by March 2026, and the draft had still not appeared in mid-May 2026. It was then cleared by the Cabinet in late June (NDTV, 29 Jun 2026) and notified on 30 June or 1 July (reports differ: ET Auto says 30 June, the Chief Minister's post says 1 July). Delhi's EV Policy 2026 is in force from 1 July 2026 to 31 March 2030 (ET Auto; electrive, 9 Jul 2026). Updated 2 October 2026.

E-buses across India

PM-eBus Sewa is a ₹57,613 crore scheme (₹20,000 crore of it central support) for 10,000 e-buses across 169 cities, with operating support for 10 years (Union Cabinet approval, Aug 2023). The scheme gives priority to cities with no organised bus service (PIB, Aug 2023).

By state: Delhi (4,845, Jul 2026), Maharashtra (4,001), Karnataka (1,989), Gujarat (1,041), Telangana (875), UP (874); figures for other states are from Vahan data, Feb 2026 (Tribune).

Surat has 450 e-buses in operation (Surat Municipal Corporation dashboard, updated 30 Apr 2026).

Citizen-led "Double the Bus" campaigns

In Mumbai, Pune, and Nagpur, citizen groups have pressured municipal budgets to prioritise bus fleet expansion over new flyovers. Policy Circle documented the campaign in September 2025. It shows informed citizens redirecting infrastructure spending toward public health.

Policy moves worth watching

CAQM 27th full commission meeting

Feb 20, 2026 (PIB)

33 domain experts submitted a meta-analysis of source apportionment studies (2015-2025) to the Supreme Court. Its main finding was that PM2.5 is the dominant pollutant in Delhi's AQI. In winter months the top contributors are secondary particulates (27%), transport (23%) and biomass burning (20%); in summer, dust is first at 27%. This moves official attention from PM10 (where improvement is easier to show) toward PM2.5 (what kills people).

Also: Direction No. 96 mandates coordinated state action plans to eliminate wheat stubble burning in 2026. CAQM is pushing barrier-free Multi-Lane Free Flow (MLFF) systems at MCD toll plazas to reduce vehicular congestion and idling emissions.

Year-round action plans (2026)

CAQM / DPCC, Feb 2026

Twelve NCR cities submitted detailed action plans in Feb 2026. Delhi targets a 15% AQI reduction and PM2.5 reduction from 99 to 96 µg/m³.

Legacy waste deadlines: Okhla by July 2026 (deadline has passed; status not checked), Bhalswa by Dec 2026, Ghazipur by Dec 2027. CAQM will periodically assess progress. The move from reactive to continuous planning matters, even though the targets are modest.

Public participation precedent

A 2023 study published in PMC analysed CAQM's Supreme Court-mandated open public comment process and documented that it influenced policy. 115 public and expert comments were compiled via RTI. Recurring themes (green transport, cycling, alternative fuels, crop burning) were incorporated into CAQM policy. It shows that public comment can shape air quality policy when the law requires it.

Industrial compliance wins

Gujarat's common boiler policy and Odisha's investment in Continuous Emission Monitoring Systems (CEMS) are described as good practices in a CSE review of clean-air action. Indore has closed its entire waste and material loop. Pune has integrated its informal workforce into service delivery. Hyderabad has adopted decentralised management with four plants under a public-private partnership model.

BS-VI emission standards

India skipped BS-V and went straight to BS-VI emission standards in April 2020, equivalent to Euro 6. This was one of the most ambitious vehicle emission standard upgrades globally. Delhi's "No PUC, No Fuel" drive added to it: over 15,000 vehicles were denied fuel in four days, 26-29 April 2026 (EPIC-India). Delhi's fuel ban on diesel vehicles over 10 years and petrol vehicles over 15 years began on 1 July 2025 but was halted by the Supreme Court's interim order of 12 August 2025, so it is not counted as a win.

What communities are winning

Children's voices / Clean Air Fund

Sesame Workshop India's "Mera Planet, Mera Ghar" project ran 12 weekly workshops for 1,600 children aged 5-10 in Delhi, reaching more than 6 million Delhi citizens (Clean Air Fund case study). Claims about Warrior Moms have been removed pending a source (source to be added).

RTI as an accountability tool

Citizen RTI applications have forced disclosure of NCAP fund spending data and public comment responses. The PMC study confirming CAQM incorporated public feedback was itself enabled by an RTI application. In our view the right to information is among the most useful tools citizens have for clean air accountability.

What the COVID lockdown showed

April 2020 remains the most powerful data point in India's clean air story. PM2.5 fell by roughly 40-50% in Indian cities (Delhi: over 50% vs pre-lockdown weeks; Mahato et al. 2020). Blue skies were visible in Delhi for weeks. The Himalayas became visible from Punjab plains for the first time in decades. It was not a policy achievement. It did show that India's air pollution is largely human-caused and can be reduced, and that knowledge fuels every citizen campaign, every RTI filing, every "Double the Bus" rally. Clean air is possible; whether it happens depends on political will.

How to use these wins

Progress is no reason to relax. It shows that action works, and that doing nothing is a choice. When a government says clean air is "too complex" or "too expensive," point them to these numbers. This is our own view: improvement is more likely where residents press for it and institutions respond, though the causes of individual cities' falls have not been established here.

For advocacy

Use Varanasi (a PM2.5 monitor-data fall) and Bareilly (a PM10 forecast) as pointers, not proof, that NCAP targets are achievable. Ask your city why it hasn't matched them.

For policy

Cite the CAQM public participation precedent in your submissions. File RTIs on your city's NCAP fund spending.

For journalism

Compare e-bus deployment pace across states. Ask why some cities achieve 70% reductions while others worsen.
