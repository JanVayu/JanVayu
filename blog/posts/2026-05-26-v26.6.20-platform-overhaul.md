# Twenty-one fixes in one day

**Published:** 26 May 2026 | **Author:** Team JanVayu | **Reading time:** 7 min

---

A domain expert tested the chatbot and found three wrong answers. A layperson said the menu was confusing. Someone pointed out that the site had no section on gender. By the end of the day we had shipped 19 changes (21 since 24 May, counting the chatbot-accuracy and menu changes merged on 24 May). Here is what changed and why.

## The chatbot was giving wrong answers

Komal works on air quality in Patna. She asked the chatbot how many monitoring stations are installed in Patna and got a wrong answer. She knew it was wrong because she had worked there.

The chatbot was drawing on WAQI, which returns only some of the stations. It knew nothing of the full CPCB network.

We gave it a reference list for 27 cities, with the number and type of stations in each: CAAQMS (continuous, real-time) or manual (gravimetric, 24-hour sampling). The Patna entry we first published is shown below.

*Correction, 2 October 2026: a fact-check found that the Patna split below is not supported. CPCB's continuous-monitoring feed lists at least five continuous (CAAQMS) stations for Patna: IGSC Planetarium Complex, Muradpur, Rajbansi Nagar, Samanpura and DRM Office Danapur. Rajbansi Nagar is a continuous station, not a manual one, and we have found no source for the other three manual station names. The entry is being re-derived from CPCB's current station list.*

```
Patna (as originally shipped, now corrected as noted above)
  CAAQMS (3): IGSC Planetarium, Muradpur, Samanpura
  Manual (4): Rajbansi Nagar, Khajpura, Gardanibagh, Phulwari Sharif
```

The list can now be edited without changing any code, so anyone on the team can correct it.

We regrouped the menu into 7 tabs by what you want to do, instead of by type of data.

## The map can now show history

The live map used to show only the current AQI. Users asked whether things were getting better or worse, and had to open a separate trends panel, pick a city and compare in their heads.

The map now has a **History** switch with a month and year slider, from January 2024 to the present. Drag the slider and the colour-coded PM2.5 markers for 12 cities change as you go. Green is the lowest band (<30 µg/m³) and purple the highest (>150 µg/m³). These are JanVayu's own colour bands, not CPCB or US EPA categories.

## Fewer things to update by hand

Seven parts of the platform now keep themselves current, so the numbers do not go stale between releases. The chatbot's station list is edited in one place. The reading list fills itself from our Zotero group, so adding a paper there is enough. The sitemap is rebuilt automatically. A daily check tests all five social feeds. A script flags out-of-date terms in the translated documents. The headline numbers on the dashboard come from a single file. The version number is kept in step across the site.

## The full count

By the end of the day:

- 19 changes were merged on 26 May (21 since 24 May).
- The site had **44 panels** (was 43), with Women's Health added.
- It had **12 roles** (was 10), with Woman/Caregiver added and Citizen split from Activist.
- The chatbot covered **33 cities** (was 10).
- 17 menu labels were reworded for clarity.
- 9 broken icons, which showed as coloured squares, were replaced.
- 8 cramped layouts, where resource cards were unreadable, were fixed.
- Every chatbot answer now has thumbs up and thumbs down buttons for feedback.

The changes are recorded in the [changelog](https://www.janvayu.in/#about), the [wiki](https://github.com/JanVayu/JanVayu/wiki) and the [roadmap](https://github.com/JanVayu/JanVayu/wiki/Roadmap).
