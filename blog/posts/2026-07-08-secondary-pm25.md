# Ammonium sulphate is up to 42% of PM2.5 in some Indian states, and it forms in the air

**Published:** 8 July 2026 | **Author:** Team JanVayu | **Reading time:** 4 min

---

When we picture air pollution, we picture a source: a truck's exhaust, a brick kiln's chimney, a field of burning stubble, a cloud of construction dust. Something is emitted and we breathe it. Yet a large share of the fine particulate matter that shortens Indian lives was never emitted as particles. It forms in the atmosphere, hours and hundreds of kilometres downwind, out of invisible gases. Two analyses by the Centre for Research on Energy and Clean Air (CREA) put a number on one part of it. Ammonium sulphate, a salt formed in the air from sulphur dioxide and ammonia, averages about **34% of India's PM2.5 mass** nationwide (June 2025 report) and reaches **42% in Chhattisgarh**, the highest of any state (December 2025 analysis, 2024 data). Other secondary particles, such as ammonium nitrate and organic aerosol, come on top of that.

That changes what "cleaning the air" has to mean.

## Primary and secondary particles

**Primary** PM2.5 is emitted as particles: soot from diesel, fly ash, road and construction dust. A filter, a cover or a water spray can be pointed at it.

**Secondary** PM2.5 starts as a gas. Sulphur dioxide (SO₂), nitrogen oxides (NOₓ) and ammonia (NH₃) leave smokestacks, tailpipes and farms, react with sunlight and with each other, and condense into fine particles such as ammonium sulphate and ammonium nitrate. By the time they count as PM2.5, the "source" is a plume of gas that left a chimney a long way away. Using NASA's MERRA-2 reanalysis for 2024, CREA finds that ammonium sulphate makes up **17% to 42%** of PM2.5 mass across Indian states (most states 30-40%), with 42% in Chhattisgarh. CREA's December press release headlines "up to 42%" of PM2.5 as chemically formed, and the figure beneath it is ammonium sulphate in Chhattisgarh, one salt in one state. Ammonium sulphate is one component, so the total secondary share is larger than any figure CREA reports for it.

## Coal, sulphur and the missing scrubbers

The main precursor in India is SO₂, and India is the **world's largest SO₂ emitter**. The biggest single contributor is coal-fired power, at least **60% of national SO₂ emissions** (as reported by [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/air/large-share-of-indias-pm25-not-emitted-directly-but-chemically-formed-in-the-atmosphere-crea-study)). That SO₂ becomes ammonium sulphate, and the pattern is regional. CREA attributes **42% of Chhattisgarh's** and **41% of Odisha's** PM2.5 to ammonium sulphate, and both states depend heavily on coal power. Even in Delhi, roughly **one-third** of PM2.5 is secondary ammonium sulphate, rising to **49% in the post-monsoon** window.

A central way to cut SO₂ is flue-gas desulphurisation (FGD), which means scrubbers on power-plant stacks. CREA says the regulatory framework now exempts about **78% of coal-fired power plants** from installing FGD. That leaves the sulphur dioxide from those plants to form ammonium sulphate downwind.

## Why "dust-first" policy falls short

Much of India's clean-air spending targets what can be seen: paving roads, spraying water, sweeping dust, greening verges. These measures are worth doing, but they act mostly on *primary* coarse particles. They do almost nothing about a sulphate particle formed in the sky from a power-plant gas. In CREA's words, "without addressing secondary particulate matter, improvements in air quality are likely to remain limited and short-lived."

Ammonium sulphate forms across a region, so a city with few local sulphur dioxide sources can still carry a large share of it: CREA's June 2025 report found it above 30% of PM2.5 in 114 of 130 NCAP cities. Fight only the pollution you can see being emitted, and in the most affected states up to about two-fifths of the PM2.5 mass is ammonium sulphate, which dust control does not reach.

## What it would take

Cutting secondary PM2.5 means regulating **gases** as well as dust. Scrubbers on coal plants would be the largest single step on SO₂, and the mandate needs enforcing rather than extending. NOₓ controls on power and transport would reduce the ammonium nitrate that forms from them. Ammonia from agriculture is a precursor that clean-air policy rarely addresses. It reacts with the acids that sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides form in the air, and the products are ammonium sulphate and ammonium nitrate. And monitoring should record what the PM2.5 is made of, not only how much there is, so that control money follows the chemistry.

## What JanVayu takes from this

We have added CREA's December 2025 secondary-PM2.5 analysis to the [Reading List](https://www.janvayu.in/#resources). It fits a theme that runs through this platform: the picture of India's air that holds up is rarely the intuitive one. A ward is not one number, and a city's PM2.5 is not one source. A great deal of what you breathe was assembled overhead, out of gases that left a chimney nobody photographed.
