# Delhi's 220 good-to-moderate days check out to within a day, and one of them was rated Good

**Published:** 5 November 2026 | **Author:** Team JanVayu | **Reading time:** 6 min

---

A post on X by the environmentalist Vimlendu Jha, which reached us on 5 November as a screenshot, says Delhi's Environment Minister claims 222 "good air days" so far this year, while the official data shows one. It says the minister reached 222 by counting Moderate days as good, and calls that institutional gaslighting and deliberate misrepresentation. We checked it against the Union Environment Ministry's release and CPCB's daily bulletins. The count is real and the one-good-day figure is right. The label on the count is the problem, and we could not find a figure of 222 anywhere.

## What the Ministry published

On 31 October the Union Environment Ministry (PIB release 2331032) reported 220 days of "Good to Moderate" air in Delhi from 1 January to 31 October 2026. It set that against 200 days in 2025, 201 in 2024, 206 in 2023, 160 in 2022, 197 in 2021, 224 in 2020, 175 in 2019 and 157 in 2018. It also reported 84 "Poor to Severe+" days against 104 in both 2025 and 2024.

Earlier figures from the same government were 211 days as of 17 October (IANS) and 214 days around 22 October (Business Today). We found no statement of 222. If the post quotes a later figure, we have not seen it.

We count from CPCB's own daily bulletin. For 1 January to 31 October, 304 days, Delhi was rated Good on 1 day, Satisfactory on 62 and Moderate on 156, which is 219 days. It was Poor on 66, Very Poor on 17 and Severe on 2, which is 85. The Ministry's 220 and 84 are one day away from ours, the same one-day gap we found on 17 October, when we counted 210 against the government's 211. The government has not published its day-by-day list, so we cannot say which day it is.

The bulletins since 31 October leave no room for more. Delhi was Poor on 1 November (261), then Very Poor on 2 November (324), 3 November (350) and 4 November (377). No day after 31 October qualifies, so no count taken from the bulletins to 4 November can reach 222.

## What is in the 220

On our count, one of the 219 days was Good, 62 were Satisfactory and 156 were Moderate. The post's "one good day" matches CPCB. Delhi's bulletin has rated between 0 and 5 days a year Good since 2016: 0, 2, 0, 2, 5, 1, 3, 1, 0, 0 and now 1 for 2016 to 2026 in order.

The post gives Moderate as 100 to 200. On CPCB's scale it is 101 to 200, and 100 is the top of Satisfactory.

## Whether the label is the problem

The Ministry's release says "Good to Moderate" and defines the category as a daily average AQI below 200. It does not call these days good. The headlines did. On 3 November outlets ran "220 clean-air days", and a Moderate day is not clean by the national standard. We set that out on 19 October: for PM2.5 the national 24-hour standard is 60 µg/m³ and a Moderate day covers 61 to 90 µg/m³.

"Deliberate misrepresentation" is a claim about intent, and the data cannot show intent. What the data shows is this. The categories are CPCB's. The release states its definition. Its comparison uses the same January-to-October window for every year, which the 17 October statement did not. A headline that reads "220 clean-air days" still tells a reader something the numbers do not say. On our count, 63 days were Satisfactory or better, the days on which the city's index was within the national standard.

## What else the release shows

The January-to-October average AQI was 167, against 170 in 2025 and 156 in 2020. Average PM2.5 from 1 January to 29 October was 69 µg/m³, against 72 in 2025 and 71 in 2020. Average PM10 was 171, against 166 in 2025 and 147 in 2020, so coarse dust went up while fine particles edged down.

2020 still has more Good-to-Moderate days than 2026 (224) and fewer Poor-to-Severe+ days (81). The release's own headline says "highest since 2018 (except 2020)", and its table agrees.

## Weather and what came next

The last days of October were wet. A news report puts rain at 61.4 mm at Safdarjung on 30 October, and CPCB rated that day Satisfactory at 60. AQI was 117 on 31 October and 261 on 1 November, and Stage II of GRAP was invoked on 2 November according to press reports. Weather moves a count of days on its own, so a count that ends in a week of rain says less about policy than it appears to.

## What would settle it

Publishing the day-by-day list behind each count would find the one-day gap and let anyone trace a figure like 222. If you have the statement that contains 222, send it to `contribute@janvayu.in` and we will add it here.
