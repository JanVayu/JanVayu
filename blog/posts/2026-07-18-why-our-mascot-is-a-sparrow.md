# Why JanVayu's Mascot Is a Sparrow

Meet **Vayu Gauraiya** — *vayu*, the air; *gauraiya*, the house sparrow. She's the face of **Ask JanVayu**, our chatbot — the little watchful friend who greets you when you come with a question. (JanVayu's own logo stays what it's always been: a green leaf carried on air currents. Vayu is the *bot's* mascot, not a rebrand of the platform.) We picked a sparrow on purpose, and not because sparrows are cute (though they are). We picked her because a sparrow is one of the most honest instruments a city has.

## A bird that measures a city

The house sparrow is a **bioindicator** — a living gauge of an ecosystem's health. Because sparrows live so close to us, in our eaves and hedges and courtyards, the state of their population quietly reports the state of the place we've built. When the sparrows thrive, the small web of life a city depends on — insects, seeds, hedges, water, nooks to nest in — is intact. When they vanish, something underneath has broken.

And in urban India, they have been vanishing. Over roughly the last three decades the house sparrow has declined sharply across our metros — Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Chennai — to the point that a bird every Indian grew up with is now a rare sight in many neighbourhoods. It mattered enough that in **2012 Delhi named the house sparrow its State Bird**, an official admission that we'd let a companion species slip away.

## The same story as the air

Here's why a sparrow belongs on an air-quality platform. The decline has several debated causes — and we won't pretend it's a simple one — but read the list and you'll recognise it:

- **Sealed modern buildings** — glass facades with no eaves, ventilators or holes to nest in, replacing the older homes and courtyards sparrows lived alongside.
- **Vanishing food** — pesticides and the loss of native hedges wiped out the insects that sparrow chicks need, and studies point to traffic-related pollutants harming those insect populations too.
- **Less green, more concrete, more traffic** — the same shift, city by city, that thickens the air we breathe.

We're careful here — nobody should claim air pollution *alone* emptied the courtyards; the honest answer is that the sparrow is a sentinel of the **whole** urban environment, air included. But that's exactly the point. **The choices that foul our air are the same choices that silenced the sparrows** — we paved the green, sealed the nooks, poisoned the insects, and filled the streets with exhaust. A city that has become unlivable for a sparrow is well on its way to being unlivable for a child.

So Vayu Gauraiya isn't decoration. She's a small, stubborn reminder that clean air isn't an abstract number on a dashboard — it's whether the living things that share your street can still make a home there.

## What she does here

Vayu lives inside **Ask JanVayu**, our chatbot — a warm, patient guide who explains your air, never invents a fact, and points you to something you can actually do about it. She sits with a breeze at her back (*vayu*) and keeps an eye on a green leaf — a nod to JanVayu's own leaf-and-air-currents logo, the emblem she works under. That green leaf she watches over is more or less the whole job description of this platform.

**World Sparrow Day** falls on 20 March — begun by the conservation group Nature Forever in 2010 to raise the alarm we'd all been ignoring. If Vayu makes you want to help the real thing: put out a shallow dish of water and some grain, leave a nesting nook unsealed, plant something native, go easy on the pesticides. And check your air on JanVayu while you're at it. It's the same act of care, pointed at the same city.

Because clean air is a right — and a city fit to breathe in is a city where the sparrows come back.
