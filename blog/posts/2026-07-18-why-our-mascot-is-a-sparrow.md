# Why JanVayu's mascot is a sparrow

**Published:** 18 July 2026 | **Author:** Team JanVayu | **Reading time:** 5 min

---

Meet **Vayu Gauraiya**: *vayu* is the air, *gauraiya* the house sparrow. She is the face of **Ask JanVayu**, our chatbot, the small watchful friend who greets you when you arrive with a question. (JanVayu's own logo stays what it has always been, a green leaf carried on air currents. Vayu is the *bot's* mascot, and the platform has not been rebranded.) We chose a sparrow on purpose, and not only because sparrows are cute. A sparrow is one of the most honest instruments a city has.

## A bird that measures a city

The house sparrow is a **bioindicator**, a living gauge of an ecosystem's health. Sparrows live close to us, in eaves, hedges and courtyards, so their numbers report on the place we have built. When sparrows do well, the small web of life a city depends on (insects, seeds, hedges, water, places to nest) is intact. When they vanish, something underneath has broken.

In urban India they have been vanishing. Over roughly the last two decades the house sparrow has declined in urban India, Delhi included (Down To Earth, 2019), until a bird every Indian grew up with is now a rare sight in many neighbourhoods. In **2012 Delhi named the house sparrow its State Bird**, an official admission that a companion species had slipped away.

## The same story as the air

The decline has several debated causes, and it is not a simple story. But the list will look familiar.

Modern buildings are sealed. Glass facades have no eaves, ventilators or holes to nest in, and they have replaced the older homes and courtyards sparrows lived beside. Food has gone too: pesticides and the loss of native hedges wiped out the insects that sparrow chicks need, and studies point to traffic-related pollutants harming those insects as well. And cities have more concrete, less green and more traffic, the same shift that thickens the air we breathe.

Nobody should claim that air pollution *alone* emptied the courtyards. The sparrow is a sentinel of the **whole** urban environment, air included. The choices that foul our air are the same choices that silenced the sparrows: we paved the green, sealed the nooks, poisoned the insects and filled the streets with exhaust. A city that has become unlivable for a sparrow is well on its way to being unlivable for a child.

So Vayu Gauraiya is more than decoration. She is a reminder that clean air is not an abstract number on a dashboard. It decides whether the living things on your street can still make a home there.

## What she does here

Vayu lives inside **Ask JanVayu**, a warm, patient guide who explains your air, never invents a fact, and points you to something you can do about it. She sits with a breeze at her back (*vayu*) and keeps an eye on a green leaf, a nod to JanVayu's own leaf-and-air-currents logo. That leaf is more or less the whole job description of this platform.

**World Sparrow Day** falls on 20 March. The conservation group Nature Forever began it in 2010 to raise the alarm. If Vayu makes you want to help the real bird, put out a shallow dish of water and some grain, leave a nesting nook unsealed, plant something native and go easy on pesticides. Check your air on JanVayu while you are at it. It is the same care, pointed at the same city.
