# You Don't Need to Code to Make India's Air Data Better

**Published:** 18 July 2026 | **Author:** Team JanVayu | **Reading time:** 4 min

---

People assume there are only two ways to help a project like JanVayu: write code, or send money. We're not taking donations right now, and most of what actually moves this project forward isn't code at all. It's a citizen in Kanpur telling us the chatbot gave a wrong answer. It's a teacher in Chennai translating a panel into Tamil. It's someone noticing a number looks off and saying so.

So we rebuilt our **[Contribute page](https://www.janvayu.in/#about)** to ask for specific, concrete help — the kind that genuinely advances the work — and to make sending it take about a minute.

## The seven asks — and why each one matters

**1. Test Vayu Gauraiya, our chatbot.** This is the single most useful thing a non-coder can do. [Ask JanVayu](https://www.janvayu.in/ask/) hard or imaginative questions — in any language — like *"how do I convince my RWA to stop burning leaves?"* If an answer **invents a fact, cites a source that doesn't exist, or just feels off**, send us the exact question and answer. We run automated quality checks on the bot every week; your real-world example becomes a test case that stops that failure from ever coming back. (More on how those routines work in [The Robots That Keep JanVayu Honest](2026-07-18-routines-that-keep-janvayu-honest.md).)

**2. Add your testimony.** Tell us, in your language, what the air is like where you live — the smell in November, the kid who coughs, the day you couldn't see the end of the street. Real voices belong on the Citizen Testimony wall, and they carry a truth no monitor does.

**3. Suggest a sourced fact** for the "Did You Know" wall. One rule: include where it's from. We never publish an unsourced number — a discipline we wrote about after [finding a citation that didn't exist](2026-07-02-citation-integrity.md) in our own site.

**4. Translate a panel** into Hindi, Tamil, Marathi or Bengali. Clean air information locked in English isn't information for most of India.

**5. Report something stale or wrong** — a figure that looks off, a broken link, an aged claim, a layout bug on your phone. You are our best staleness detector; our automated sweep is the second line, not the first.

**6. Write code.** If you *do* code, pick a [good first issue](https://github.com/JanVayu/JanVayu/labels/good%20first%20issue) and start with [CONTRIBUTING.md](https://github.com/JanVayu/JanVayu/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md). The whole platform is open source.

**7. Run our social media.** We're deliberately not on social yet — better nowhere than badly. If you'd enjoy running it well, join the team and lead it.

## Where it goes

Every one of these flows to the **same place**: the contribute form on the [Contribute page](https://www.janvayu.in/#about). Fill it in — name, email, what you'd like to help with, and a message — and it lands two ways at once: an email to a real person on the team, and a stored record so nothing is lost. Prefer plain email? Write to [contribute@janvayu.in](mailto:contribute@janvayu.in). Either way, a human reads it and replies.

That's the whole point of building the form the way we did. A "get involved!" button that goes nowhere is worse than no button. This one goes somewhere.

## Why we ask so specifically

Vague calls for help get vague help. "Support us!" makes people feel guilty and do nothing. "Ask the chatbot a hard question in your mother tongue and tell us if it lies" is something a person can actually do on their commute home — and it makes JanVayu measurably better the same week.

We're a tiny volunteer team. We can't do everything. But a hundred people each doing one specific, concrete thing — that's a movement, and it's exactly how a citizen-led air-quality record stays honest, current, and worth trusting.

Clean air is a right. Keeping the record of it honest is a job. Come do a piece of it with us.
