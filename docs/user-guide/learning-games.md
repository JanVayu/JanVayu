# Learning games

JanVayu's `/#games` panel holds seven self-paced educational games, all written with original content set in India. Open them from Action → Learning Games in the menu, or go straight to [janvayu.in/#games](https://www.janvayu.in/#games).

For a live session led by a host, including Dr. Sarath Guttikunda's longer Jeopardy run with audience teams, request a booking from the [Workshops panel](https://www.janvayu.in/#workshops).

---

## 1. Air Quality Jeopardy, India edition

A classic Jeopardy quiz board with Indian content.

The board has twenty-five tiles in five categories (Sources, Health, Policy, Cities, Action), each with five tiles worth ₹1,000 to ₹5,000. Click a tile to see a statement. The statement is the answer, and you think of the question it answers. Reveal the official answer, mark whether you got it, and earn the rupee value of the tile.

For example, a tile might read: *"This Indian city was named the most polluted capital in the world in IQAir 2025."* The matching question is *"What is New Delhi?"*

The top score is ₹75,000, for clearing all 25 tiles. Scores stay on your own device, and the site does not log results.

Each clue comes with a one-paragraph educational note drawn from CEEW 2024, IIT-Delhi DSS apportionment, NCAP records, IQAir 2025 and the Lancet Countdown 2025.

The game follows the long-running classroom Air Quality Jeopardy that Dr. Sarath Guttikunda runs live at UrbanEmissions.info.

## 2. PM Quick-Quiz

Ten multiple-choice questions, about 3 minutes in all. Each answer is followed by a short note on why the right answer is right and why the other options are wrong.

The questions cover:
- PM2.5 basics (what the number means)
- The WHO 2021 guideline (5 µg/m³)
- Lancet Countdown 2025 mortality (1.72M)
- GRAP stage thresholds (201 / 301 / 401 / 451)
- Berkeley Earth cigarette equivalence (1 cig ≈ 22 µg/m³·day)
- IQAir 2025 city rankings (Loni, Delhi, Byrnihat)
- The gap between NAAQS and WHO (8×)
- How CPCB, SPCB and CAAQMS fit together
- The mix of PM2.5 sources in India
- The stubble burning calendar

## 3. Source Matcher

Seven main sources of PM2.5 in India, each paired with a one-sentence description that holds a fact a casual reader would not already know.

Tap a source on the left, then tap the matching description on the right. Wrong matches are caught when you click "Check", and "Shuffle & reset" mixes the cards for another round.

The sources are stubble burning, residential biomass cooking, coal-fired thermal power, road dust and non-exhaust emissions, brick kilns, diesel generators and open waste burning. The descriptions come from the CEEW 2024 source-apportionment review.

## 4. Clean Air Snakes & Ladders (Moksha Patam edition)

A 6×6 board of 36 squares on the classic winding path. Press "Roll dice" to move 1–6 squares forward.

There are 5 ladders, each a positive citizen action that improves the air: chulha to LPG (square 3 to 11), a fit-tested N95 (7 to 17), an RTI on NCAP fund spending (14 to 24), a public comment on the draft City Action Plan (21 to 30), and joining the RWA pollution committee (27 to 35).

There are 5 snakes, each a pollution event or policy slip: Diwali fireworks (10 to 2), a missed FGD deadline (18 to 6), the stubble-burning peak (25 to 13), a GRAP-IV trigger (32 to 19) and an NCAP deadline slip (34 to 20).

Each special square shows a one-line fact. The goal is square 36, *"India meets the WHO 5 µg/m³ guideline."* The fewer the rolls, the closer you are to the ideal path.

The game is modelled on *Moksha Patam*, the original Indian Snakes & Ladders.

## 5. Jodi Match (memory cards)

Twelve face-down cards make six matching pairs (*jodis*). Tap any two. If they form a pair, both stay face-up, and otherwise both flip back. Clear all six pairs in as few moves as you can. A perfect run takes six moves.

The pairs teach core associations:

| Side A | Side B |
|---|---|
| PM2.5 | Cooking smoke (chulha biomass) |
| NCAP | National Clean Air Programme |
| GRAP-IV | AQI > 450 (severe-plus) |
| CAQM | NCR statutory air-quality body (2021 Act) |
| WHO PM2.5 | 5 µg/m³ annual guideline (since 2021) |
| N95 | Mask filtering ≥95% of PM2.5 when fitted |

It is the memory-card game (Pelmanism) that most Indian households already know.

## 6. Air Tambola (Indian housie)

A classic tambola ticket of 3 rows and 9 columns, with 15 air quality terms in place of the numbers 1–90. The terms come from a pool of 27 (PM2.5, GRAP-IV, NCAP, CAAQMS, Loni, Stubble, FGD, N95, HEPA, BS-VI, AQLI, Lancet 1.72M, RTI, Black Carbon, Brick Kiln, ozone, NOx, SO₂ and others).

Press "Call next" and a one-line clue is read out, for example *"India's continuous air quality monitoring station network, a six-letter acronym."* If the answer is on your ticket, tap it to mark it.

The game detects all four classic Indian housie wins:

- Top Line: all five cells in row 1 marked
- Middle Line: all five cells in row 2 marked
- Bottom Line: all five cells in row 3 marked
- Full House: every cell on the ticket marked

A cold ticket finishes inside 27 calls.

## 7. Vayu Junction (new in v26.6.0)

A word-grouping puzzle inspired by BBC's *Only Connect*, the NYT *Connections* daily and the [*Torchlight*](https://timesofclimatechange.com/torchlight/) climate puzzle at Times of Climate Change, built around India's air quality vocabulary.

Sixteen tiles sit on a 4×4 grid, in four hidden groups of four. Tap four tiles you think share a hidden connection and hit "Submit". A correct guess locks the four in a colour, with the theme revealed. A wrong guess costs a strike, and after four strikes the puzzle reveals itself. If three of your four belong together, the game says so ("swap one"), which makes the feedback more useful.

Four original puzzle sets came with the launch:

| # | Title | Difficulty | Sample groups |
|---|-------|-----------|---------------|
| 1 | Basics | Easy | Particulate fractions · Criteria gases · CPCB AQI bands · Indian regulators |
| 2 | Sources, seasons & protection | Medium | Combustion sources · Smog-season months · GRAP stages · Mask & filter terms |
| 3 | Names & numbers | Hard | Worst-polluted Indian cities · NCAP top performers · AQ research bodies · Citizen-action tools |
| 4 | Devious | Devious | Types of "___ carbon" · BS emission standards · Citizen acronyms · PM-precursor gases |

Solved groups stack above the live grid in their theme colour. Scores are not saved or sent anywhere, and there is no leaderboard.
