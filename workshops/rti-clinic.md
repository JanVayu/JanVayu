# RTI clinic

Forty-five minutes. You will leave having filed a real Right to Information
request about air quality in your own city.

Not a draft. A filed one.

Bring the name of your city and, if you can, the name of your state pollution
control board. Everything else is on the screen.

# What an RTI can actually get you

The Right to Information Act, 2005 lets any Indian citizen ask any public
authority for information it holds. You do not have to explain why you want it.
Section 6(2) says so explicitly.

The application fee is **₹10** for central public authorities under the RTI
Rules, 2012, and nothing at all if you hold a BPL card. State authorities set
their own fee by state rules and most, though not all, have kept it at ₹10;
check your own state's rules before sending a postal order for the wrong amount.

The reply is due within **30 days** (Section 7(1)). Where the information
concerns the life or liberty of a person it is due within **48 hours**.

For air quality, the useful thing is that almost everything you want to know is
already written down by somebody:

- What the monitors in your city actually recorded, hour by hour, and how often
  they were working at all
- How much NCAP money your city was given, how much it spent, and on what
- Which industries and brick kilns near you hold a valid Consent to Operate, and
  which do not
- Whether anyone was penalised when the air went past the emergency thresholds
- How many school days your city lost to pollution closures

None of that is secret. It is simply not published, which is a different
problem with a legal remedy.

# Choose your question

Do not ask everything. One focused application gets answered; a sprawling one
gets a partial answer months late.

Be precise about the law here, because it is often stated wrongly. **Section
7(9) is not a ground for refusing information.** It only lets a PIO give you the
information in a different *form* from the one you asked for, if your form would
disproportionately divert resources. The grounds for actually withholding
information are in Sections 8 and 9, and "this is too much work" is not among
them. In practice, though, a request with fifteen limbs invites a partial
response and an appeal you then have to fight, so keep it to one subject.

Pick **one** of these. Mark this step done when you have.

**1. The monitors.** Is your city's network actually working? You ask for
per-station uptime, calibration schedules and data-quality audit reports. This
is the strongest opening request because the April 2025 CAG audit already found
88% of CPCB stations had at least one data-quality issue in 2023-24, so you are
asking about a known problem.

**2. The money.** What did your city get under NCAP and what did it spend?
You ask for the tranche-wise release, the category-wise utilisation, the
unutilised balance, and copies of the utilisation certificates sent to MoEFCC.
Add the tender list with vendor names and amounts. Note that commercial
confidence is an exemption under Section 8(1)(d), so expect a fight over vendor
terms, though not over the amounts a public authority has disbursed.

**3. The polluters.** Which units near you are operating with consent and which
are not? You ask for the list within your pincode or ward, their self-monitoring
data, and what enforcement action was taken in the last 24 months.

**4. The schools.** How many days did children lose? You ask for the closure
orders, the days lost in government schools, what was arranged instead, and the
budget set aside for next winter.

# Draft it

Open the **RTI Assistant** on JanVayu. From a City Scorecard there is a one-click
"File an RTI" that pre-fills your state board and the Clean Air Action Plan
topic.

The template gives you four things, and each matters:

**The right Public Information Officer.** An RTI sent to the wrong authority is
not refused, it is transferred under Section 6(3), and that costs you five days.
Monitoring and consent questions go to the State Pollution Control Board. NCAP
money goes to the SPCB with a copy to CPCB. School closures go to the state
Directorate of Education. GRAP enforcement in Delhi-NCR goes to CAQM at Vayu
Bhawan, Dwarka.

**Questions phrased as requests for records.** "Why is the air so bad?" is an
opinion and will be refused. "Provide the hourly PM2.5 data for the past 90 days
from each station, in CSV or PDF" is a record and cannot be.

**The statutory anchor.** Citing the Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution)
Act, 1981 or the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 alongside the RTI Act tells
the PIO which file to pull.

**Bracketed fields you must fill.** [CITY], [STATE], [PINCODE], [DISTRICT].
Leave one in and you will get a clarification request instead of an answer.

> **Do this now.** Fill in every bracketed field. Read your questions aloud. If
> a question could be answered "we do not hold an opinion on that", rewrite it
> as a request for a document.

# File it

Two routes.

**Online**, at [rtionline.gov.in](https://rtionline.gov.in), for central
authorities and for the states that have joined the portal. Pay the ₹10 there.
You get a registration number immediately. Keep it.

**By post**, for state authorities not on the portal. A postal order or demand
draft for ₹10 made out to the Accounts Officer of that authority. Send it by
registered post or speed post and keep the receipt, because the 30 days run from
delivery.

> **Do this now, before the session ends.** File it. A drafted RTI that stays in
> a folder is the most common outcome of a clinic like this, and it is the one
> we are trying to avoid.

Mark this step done once you have a registration number or a posting receipt.

# What happens next

**Within 30 days** you should get a reply. It may be the information, a refusal
citing a specific exemption under Section 8 or 9, or a transfer notice under
Section 6(3). A refusal that cites no provision is not a valid refusal.

**If nothing comes, or the reply is evasive**, you have a First Appeal under
Section 19(1) to the First Appellate Authority of the same public authority,
within 30 days of receiving the decision or of the time limit expiring. It is
free. Silence is itself a deemed refusal under Section 7(2), so an appeal does
not need a letter to appeal against. No form is prescribed; a letter naming your
original application and what was not answered is enough.

**If the First Appeal fails**, a Second Appeal goes under Section 19(3) to the
Central or State Information Commission within 90 days. The Commission can impose a penalty of
₹250 per day on the PIO, up to ₹25,000, under Section 20.

**If you get the data, send it to us.** A dataset that exists only in one
person's inbox helps nobody. Write to contribute@janvayu.in and we will publish
it with attribution, or help you read it if the format is awkward.
